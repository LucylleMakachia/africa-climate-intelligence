# Day 7 — Week 1 Review and Scope Boundaries

* The problem
* Potential users
* Initial geography
* Initial use cases
* The boundaries of the project

The project is currently at the **problem-validation stage**. No major technical or commercial commitments should be made until the key assumptions have been tested with users.

# 3. What We Will NOT Build — For Now

## 3.1 Not Another Generic Climate-Data Warehouse

We will not attempt to collect and store every available weather and climate dataset.

### Reason

Platforms such as KMD, ICPAC, Copernicus, NASA and the World Bank already provide substantial datasets and data infrastructure.

The potential opportunity appears more likely to involve **organization, integration, interpretation and workflow support** than simply adding another large repository.

---

## 3.2 Not a Replacement for KMD

We will not attempt to replace Kenya Meteorological Department's national meteorological services.

### Reason

KMD already provides national weather and climate services, observations, forecasts, climate information, data management and Maprooms.

Our project should investigate how existing information can potentially be made more useful to specific users rather than competing with the national meteorological service.

---

## 3.3 Not a Replacement for ICPAC

We will not attempt to recreate ICPAC's regional climate-data infrastructure.

### Reason

ICPAC already provides extensive climate, environmental, agricultural and hazard information and geospatial services across Eastern Africa.

The project may eventually integrate or reference appropriate ICPAC information where licensing and technical conditions allow.

---

## 3.4 Not a Replacement for NDMA or NEMA

We will not attempt to recreate Kenya's existing drought, environmental or early-warning institutional systems.

### Reason

These institutions already provide important information products and services.

The project should investigate potential gaps between information availability and user workflows rather than duplicate institutional functions.

---

## 3.5 Not an Africa-Wide Platform in the MVP

The initial product will focus on **Kenya**.

We will not attempt to build for all African countries simultaneously.

### Reason

Different countries have different:

* Data providers
* Institutions
* Regulations
* Languages
* Data availability
* Climate conditions
* User needs
* Licensing arrangements

Kenya provides a manageable environment for research, testing and validation.

Expansion can be considered after the initial concept has been validated.

---

## 3.6 Not an AI-First Product

We will not begin by adding AI simply because it is technically possible.

### Reason

AI should only be introduced where it solves a validated user problem.

Potential future applications could include summarization, anomaly detection or natural-language interfaces, but these will be evaluated only after the underlying user need is established.

---

## 3.7 Not a Farmer-Focused Product in the Initial MVP

Farmers remain a potentially important future user group.

However, they will not be the primary design target at this stage.

### Reason

A farmer-focused platform would require additional research into:

* Agricultural decision-making
* Crops and seasons
* Language
* Connectivity
* Mobile access
* Trust
* Extension services
* Local interpretation of forecasts
* Distribution and adoption

This is potentially a separate product direction.

---

## 3.8 Not Every Possible User at Once

We will not attempt to design simultaneously for:

* Researchers
* Farmers
* Governments
* NGOs
* Consultants
* Developers
* Businesses
* Insurers
* Financial institutions
* Universities
* General public

### Reason

Different users have fundamentally different requirements.

The project will identify and validate a primary user/problem before expanding the scope.

---

## 3.9 Not a Full Dashboard Immediately

We will not build a large production dashboard during the research stage.

### Reason

We do not yet know:

* Which indicators users actually need
* Which workflows are most valuable
* Which data sources should be used
* Which geographic scale matters
* What users are willing to pay for
* What users trust

A prototype should follow evidence rather than precede it.

---

## 3.10 Not a Paid Product Before Value Validation

We will investigate potential revenue models, but we will not assume users will pay simply because the platform exists.

### Reason

The project must first establish:

**Problem → User → Value → Willingness to use → Willingness to pay**

Only then should pricing and monetization become major product decisions.

---

# 4. Technical Scope Boundary

At the end of Week 1, the project will focus on learning and validation before committing to:

* Production database architecture
* Large-scale cloud infrastructure
* Complex microservices
* Advanced machine learning
* Large automated data pipelines
* Commercial APIs
* High-volume infrastructure
* Mobile applications

Simple technical experiments may still be created when they help answer a research question.

---

# 5. Business Scope Boundary

We will investigate potential business models, but we will not yet commit to:

* Subscription pricing
* Enterprise contracts
* Advertising
* Data licensing
* Paid APIs
* Consulting packages
* Premium dashboards
* Sponsorships

These are future hypotheses to evaluate.

---

# 6. The Core Principle for the Next Phase

The project will follow:

**Research → Validate → Define → Prototype → Test → Build → Scale**

rather than:

**Idea → Build → Hope people use it**

This means user evidence should increasingly determine the product direction.

---

# 7. Week 1 Key Decision

The project will **not** attempt to solve the entire climate-information problem.

Instead, the next phase will identify a specific, recurring and valuable problem experienced by a clearly defined user group in Kenya.

The MVP will only be defined after sufficient evidence has been gathered.

---

# 8. What We Carry Into Week 2

### Established

* Kenya is the initial geography.
* Kenya has substantial existing climate-information infrastructure.
* Multiple user groups may benefit from improved access, integration or interpretation.
* Humanitarian/development organizations are the current primary organizational hypothesis.
* Three initial use-case hypotheses have been identified.
* Existing platforms should be studied and, where appropriate, complemented rather than unnecessarily duplicated.

### Still unvalidated

* Which user has the strongest problem.
* Which workflow is most painful.
* How frequently the problem occurs.
* How much time or money it costs.
* Whether existing tools are inadequate for the specific workflow.
* What users would consider valuable.
* Whether users would pay for a solution.
* Which use case should become the MVP.
