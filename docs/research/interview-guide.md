# Kenya Climate Information — User Interview Guide

## Purpose

The purpose of these interviews is to understand how people currently obtain, process, interpret, and use weather and climate information.

The interviews should investigate existing behavior rather than attempt to persuade participants to use a proposed platform.

---

## Opening

Thank the participant and explain:

> We are researching how organizations and professionals currently use weather and climate information in Kenya. We are interested in your existing workflow, challenges, and needs. There are no right or wrong answers.

Do not begin by describing the proposed platform in detail.

---

## Section 1 — Background

1. What is your role?
2. What type of organization do you work for?
3. What types of decisions or activities involve weather or climate information?

---

## Section 2 — Recent Experience

4. Tell me about the most recent time you needed weather or climate information for your work.
5. What were you trying to find out?
6. What geographic area were you interested in?
7. What information did you need?

---

## Section 3 — Current Workflow

8. Where did you obtain the information?
9. Which websites, datasets, tools, or organizations did you use?
10. Did you use more than one source?
11. How did you combine the information?
12. What software did you use?
13. Did you need help from another person or specialist?

---

## Section 4 — Difficulties

14. What was the most difficult part?
15. Was anything difficult to find?
16. Was anything difficult to access?
17. Did you encounter problems with data formats?
18. Did you encounter problems with geographic or temporal resolution?
19. Was the information easy to interpret?
20. How much time did the process take?

---

## Section 5 — Consequences

21. What happened because of the difficulty?
22. Did it delay a decision, report, project, or activity?
23. Did you have to use an alternative source?
24. Did you ever decide not to use climate information because obtaining or processing it was too difficult?

---

## Section 6 — Existing Solutions

25. What tools or services currently make this process easier?
26. What do you like about them?
27. What do you dislike about them?
28. What do you still have to do manually?

---

## Section 7 — Improvement

29. If you could change one thing about the current process, what would it be?
30. What information would you like to receive automatically?
31. What would make climate information more useful to your work?

---

## Section 8 — Commercial / Organizational Value

Only ask these questions after understanding the current workflow.

32. Does your organization currently pay for climate, weather, GIS, environmental, or risk information?
33. What types of climate-information services does the organization pay for?
34. What makes a data or information service worth paying for?
35. Who would normally approve spending on such a service?

Avoid asking:

> "Would you pay for our platform?"

Instead investigate what the organization already spends money on and why.

---

## Interviewer Notes

### Important observation

Record what the participant **actually does**, not only what they say they would do.

### Avoid leading questions

Do not say:

> "Do you find climate data difficult to access?"

Prefer:

> "Tell me about the last time you needed climate data. What happened?"

### Look for evidence of:

* Repeated manual work
* Multiple data sources
* Time spent searching
* Data cleaning
* GIS processing
* Missing information
* Poor documentation
* Difficult interpretation
* Repeated reporting
* Monitoring needs
* Existing spending
* Workarounds
* Consequences of information gaps
