# Potential Users and Problems

## Purpose

This document maps potential users of the Africa Climate Intelligence platform and the climate/weather information problems they may experience.

At this stage, these are **hypotheses for investigation**, not established findings. They will be validated through desk research, existing-platform analysis, and user research.

The initial geographic focus is **Kenya**.

---

# 1. Humanitarian Organizations

### Potential users

* UN agencies
* International NGOs
* Local NGOs
* Humanitarian coordination teams
* Emergency-response organizations

### Potential problems to investigate

* Climate and hazard information may be distributed across multiple sources.
* Teams may need to combine climate, hazard, population, and geographic information.
* Information may not be available at the geographic level required for operations.
* Monitoring changing conditions may require repeated manual checks.
* Producing maps, dashboards, and situation reports may require specialist GIS/data skills.

### Potential platform need

Location-based climate and hazard monitoring, integrated datasets, maps, indicators, and reporting tools.

---

# 2. County Governments

### Potential users

* County planning departments
* Environment departments
* Agriculture departments
* Disaster-risk-management teams
* Water departments

### Potential problems to investigate

* Climate information may be difficult to translate into county- or ward-level planning information.
* Different departments may rely on different datasets and information systems.
* Historical climate information may be difficult to compare with current conditions.
* Producing spatial climate analysis may require specialist GIS skills.
* Information may need to be converted into reports for decision-makers.

### Potential platform need

County-level climate profiles, maps, trends, indicators, and monitoring dashboards.

---

# 3. Farmers and Agricultural Organizations

### Potential users

* Farmers
* Farmer cooperatives
* Agricultural organizations
* Agricultural extension services
* Agribusinesses

### Potential problems to investigate

* Weather forecasts may be difficult to interpret for specific agricultural decisions.
* Climate conditions can vary significantly across locations.
* Historical rainfall and temperature information may be difficult to obtain or interpret.
* Climate information may need to be combined with crop, soil, or geographic information.
* Users may need information presented in a simpler and more actionable format.

### Potential platform need

Location-specific weather and climate information connected to agricultural planning and monitoring.

---

# 4. GIS and Environmental Consultants

### Potential users

* GIS consultants
* Environmental consultants
* Spatial analysts
* Environmental assessment teams
* Climate-risk consultants

### Potential problems to investigate

* Finding appropriate climate datasets can take considerable time.
* Climate datasets may require significant preprocessing.
* Data may be provided in different formats and coordinate systems.
* Combining climate data with administrative or project boundaries may require manual GIS work.
* Dataset documentation and metadata may vary between sources.

### Potential platform need

Searchable datasets, standardized spatial data, metadata, automated processing, and GIS-ready outputs.

---

# 5. Development Organizations

### Potential users

* Development NGOs
* Programme teams
* Monitoring and evaluation teams
* Climate-resilience programmes
* Livelihoods programmes

### Potential problems to investigate

* Climate information may be difficult to incorporate into programme design.
* Programme staff may depend on technical specialists for analysis.
* Climate indicators may need to be repeatedly generated for project locations.
* Reports may require maps, charts, and historical comparisons.
* Climate information may not be presented in a form that non-specialists can easily interpret.

### Potential platform need

Project-area climate profiles, automated indicators, visualizations, and reporting tools.

---

# 6. Researchers and Universities

### Potential users

* Climate researchers
* Environmental scientists
* Geographers
* Agricultural researchers
* Development researchers
* University students

### Potential problems to investigate

* Discovering appropriate datasets can be time-consuming.
* Dataset documentation and metadata may vary.
* Research may require combining several data sources.
* Data preparation and preprocessing may be repetitive.
* Reproducing analyses may be difficult when workflows are highly manual.

### Potential platform need

Climate-data catalogue, metadata, standardized datasets, APIs, visualization, and reproducible analysis workflows.

---

# 7. Infrastructure and Engineering Organizations

### Potential users

* Infrastructure companies
* Engineering firms
* Telecommunications companies
* Road and transport organizations
* Energy companies
* Construction companies

### Potential problems to investigate

* Climate conditions can affect infrastructure planning and maintenance.
* Climate information may need to be connected to specific project locations.
* Historical extremes may be more relevant than climate averages.
* Climate-risk analysis may require specialist GIS and data skills.
* Organizations may need information for multiple project locations.

### Potential platform need

Site-specific climate profiles, historical extremes, spatial analysis, and climate monitoring.

---

# 8. Financial and Insurance Organizations

### Potential users

* Insurance companies
* Banks
* Investors
* Development-finance institutions
* Risk-management teams

### Potential problems to investigate

* Climate risk needs to be assessed geographically.
* Historical climate and hazard information may need to be linked to assets or projects.
* Different datasets may make comparisons difficult.
* Organizations may require standardized and repeatable climate indicators.
* Climate information may need to be integrated into existing risk-analysis workflows.

### Potential platform need

Location-based climate-risk information, standardized indicators, asset analysis, and monitoring.

---

# 9. Water-Resource Organizations

### Potential users

* Water utilities
* Water-resource authorities
* Basin organizations
* Hydrologists
* Water planners

### Potential problems to investigate

* Rainfall, temperature, and hydrological information may come from different sources.
* Historical variability may be difficult to analyze consistently.
* Extreme rainfall and drought conditions require monitoring.
* Climate information may need to be analyzed at watershed or catchment scale.
* Combining climate and geographic information may require specialist tools.

### Potential platform need

Rainfall, drought, temperature, and watershed-level climate monitoring and analysis.

---

# 10. Climate-Sensitive Businesses

### Potential users

Businesses whose operations are affected by weather or climate, including:

* Agriculture
* Logistics
* Tourism
* Construction
* Energy
* Food supply chains
* Natural-resource businesses

### Potential problems to investigate

* Generic weather information may not answer specific operational questions.
* Historical climate information may be difficult to access.
* Organizations may lack internal climate or GIS expertise.
* Businesses operating across multiple locations may need centralized monitoring.
* Climate information may need to be translated into operational indicators.

### Potential platform need

Automated location monitoring, climate profiles, alerts, dashboards, and organization-specific analysis.

# Cross-Cutting Problem Areas

Across the ten potential user groups, several recurring problem categories have emerged.

## 1. Data Discovery

**Question to investigate:**

> How difficult is it for users to find the climate information they actually need?

---

## 2. Data Access

**Question to investigate:**

> How easily can users obtain the required data?

Potential issues include:

* Download procedures
* APIs
* Registration
* Access restrictions
* File sizes
* Technical requirements

---

## 3. Data Integration

**Question to investigate:**

> How difficult is it to combine climate/weather data with other datasets?

Potential examples:

* Administrative boundaries
* Population
* Infrastructure
* Agriculture
* Hazards
* Project locations
* Watersheds

---

## 4. Spatial Resolution

**Question to investigate:**

> Is climate information available at the geographic scale users actually need?

Examples:

* Country
* County
* Sub-county
* Ward
* Catchment
* Project site

---

## 5. Temporal Resolution

**Question to investigate:**

> Is information available at the time scale users require?

Examples:

* Daily
* Weekly
* Monthly
* Seasonal
* Annual
* Historical
* Near-real-time

---

## 6. Interpretation

**Question to investigate:**

> Can users understand what the climate information means for their particular location or activity?

---

## 7. Monitoring

**Question to investigate:**

> How do users currently monitor changing weather and climate conditions?

---

## 8. Reporting

**Question to investigate:**

> How much manual work is required to turn climate information into maps, dashboards, reports, or briefings?

---

## 9. Technical Capacity

**Question to investigate:**

> What technical skills are required to obtain, process, analyze, and interpret the information?

---

## 10. Decision Usefulness

**Question to investigate:**

> Does the information help users make a decision, or does it primarily provide data that users must interpret themselves?

---

# Initial Research Hypothesis

The preliminary hypothesis is:

> **The challenge may not primarily be a lack of climate and weather data. Instead, users may experience difficulty discovering, accessing, integrating, interpreting, and continuously monitoring distributed climate information for specific locations and decisions.**

This hypothesis must be tested through evidence.

---
