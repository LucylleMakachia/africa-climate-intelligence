# 2. Problem Being Investigated

Kenya already has a substantial ecosystem of weather, climate, hazard and environmental information.

Relevant systems and institutions include:

* Kenya Meteorological Department (KMD)
* IGAD Climate Prediction and Applications Centre (ICPAC)
* National Drought Management Authority (NDMA)
* National Environment Management Authority (NEMA)
* Global climate-data platforms such as Copernicus
* NASA Earth-observation systems
* World Bank climate-information resources
* Academic and research datasets

The initial research therefore does **not** support the simple assumption that Kenya lacks climate information.

Instead, the project is investigating a more specific problem:

> **Climate and weather information is increasingly available through multiple systems, but availability and access do not necessarily translate into effective, location-specific and decision-relevant use.**

Potential challenges may occur across several stages:

**Discovery → Access → Integration → Analysis → Interpretation → Communication → Decision-making**

The project will investigate where the greatest user difficulties actually occur.

# 3. Evidence Gathered During Week 1

Initial research examined:

### Kenyan information ecosystem

* Kenya Meteorological Department
* ICPAC
* NDMA
* NEMA

### Academic evidence

Research concerning climate-information access, trust, interpretation and usefulness among Kenyan users, including agricultural communities.

### Existing global platforms

* KMD
* ICPAC
* Copernicus Climate Data Store
* NASA Giovanni
* World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal

### Key emerging findings

1. Kenya already has substantial climate-information infrastructure.
2. Climate datasets and information products are distributed across multiple institutions and platforms.
3. Different platforms serve different user groups and levels of technical expertise.
4. Large global platforms provide sophisticated data, analysis and API capabilities.
5. Existing evidence indicates that information availability does not necessarily guarantee effective use.
6. User trust, interpretation, applicability, accessibility and workflow integration may influence whether climate information becomes useful for decisions.
7. Data licensing and usage conditions need to be considered before incorporating third-party datasets into a commercial product.
8. There may be an opportunity around organizing and transforming existing information into more accessible, location-specific and decision-oriented workflows.

These findings remain subject to further validation.

May need to discover, access, understand and analyze climate and weather datasets.

# 6. Initial Geography

## Kenya

Kenya is the initial geographic focus because:

* It provides a manageable research environment.
* It has a developed but distributed climate-information ecosystem.
* Relevant government and regional institutions are identifiable.
* There is substantial climate, hazard and geospatial information to investigate.
* Kenya provides an opportunity to study how existing information is accessed and used rather than assuming information is absent.
* A Kenya-first approach allows the project to test the concept before considering wider African expansion.

### Pilot geography

A specific county or group of counties has **not yet been selected**.

The pilot area should be selected later using evidence such as:

* User presence
* Climate/hazard relevance
* Availability of suitable datasets
* Development/humanitarian activity
* Diversity of use cases
* Data accessibility
* Feasibility of user research

# 7. Initial Use Cases

The following are preliminary use-case hypotheses.

## Use Case 1 — Location Monitoring

### Purpose

Allow a user to monitor weather, climate and relevant hazard conditions for a specific project or geographic location.

### Potential workflow

**Select location → Select period → View indicators → View trends → Review relevant hazards → Export/share**

### Potential information

* Rainfall
* Temperature
* Forecasts
* Historical trends
* Drought indicators
* Flood information
* Other relevant hazards

### Validation required

Determine which indicators users actually need, how frequently they monitor them, and which existing sources they currently use.

---

## Use Case 2 — Climate + GIS Analysis

### Purpose

Help users combine climate and environmental information with geographic or project data.

### Potential workflow

**Select/upload project locations → Select climate indicator → Select time period → Perform analysis → Generate map/data output**

### Potential outputs

* Maps
* Spatial analysis
* Climate overlays
* Project-location analysis
* GIS-ready datasets
* Downloadable results

### Validation required

Determine which workflows users actually perform, which datasets they combine, how much manual processing is involved, and whether automation would provide meaningful value.

---

## Use Case 3 — Climate Information Reporting

### Purpose

Help users transform climate information into understandable outputs for programme teams, managers and decision-makers.

### Potential workflow

**Select location → Select period → Generate indicators/charts/maps → Produce summary → Export/share**

### Potential outputs

* Charts
* Maps
* Climate indicators
* Location summaries
* Reports

### Validation required

Determine who currently prepares these outputs, how frequently they are produced, what information decision-makers require, and how much manual effort is involved.

---

# 8. Current Product Hypothesis

The current hypothesis is:

> **Humanitarian and development organizations working in Kenya may benefit from a platform that brings together relevant weather, climate, hazard and geospatial information and makes it easier to discover, integrate, interpret and use for location-specific planning, monitoring, analysis and reporting.**

This is a hypothesis rather than a confirmed product requirement.

---

# 9. What the Platform Is NOT Assumed to Be

The project is not currently assuming that it should:

* Create new weather data
* Replace KMD
* Replace ICPAC
* Replace NDMA
* Replace NEMA
* Become another global climate-data warehouse
* Serve every possible climate-information user
* Build AI features immediately
* Provide automated climate-risk advice without appropriate evidence
* Cover all African countries in the MVP

The initial objective is to identify a specific, validated user problem that existing systems do not adequately solve.

---

# 10. Key Assumptions Requiring Validation

The following assumptions must be tested during subsequent research.

### User assumptions

1. Humanitarian and development organizations experience a significant recurring problem when using climate/weather information.
2. GIS, information-management, programme and M&E teams encounter this problem.
3. The problem occurs frequently enough to justify a dedicated solution.

### Workflow assumptions

4. Users currently obtain information from multiple sources.
5. Combining these sources creates meaningful friction.
6. Users spend significant time searching, cleaning, processing or interpreting information.
7. A more integrated workflow could save time or improve decision-making.

### Product assumptions

8. Users would value location-specific climate intelligence rather than raw data alone.
9. Users would trust information if source, methodology and provenance were clearly shown.
10. Existing datasets can legally and technically be incorporated into a new platform.

### Business assumptions

11. The problem is sufficiently valuable that organizations may pay for a solution.
12. A sustainable business model can be developed without restricting essential public-interest information.
13. The platform can provide value beyond existing free government and international services.

None of these assumptions should be treated as proven yet.

# 11. Week 1 Research Questions

The next phase should investigate:

1. Which user group experiences the strongest version of the problem?
2. What exact workflow causes the greatest difficulty?
3. How much time and effort does the current workflow require?
4. Which information sources are currently used?
5. What information is missing, difficult to access or difficult to interpret?
6. What existing tools do users combine?
7. What outputs do users need?
8. What would make users trust a new platform?
9. What would make an organization pay for it?
10. Which potential use case should become the MVP?
